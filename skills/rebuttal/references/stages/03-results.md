# 3. Interpret the results and update the claims

Accept partial results, counterexamples, collaborator summaries and failed runs. On direct entry, ask what claim the material is meant to support only if the supplied question leaves that ambiguous.

1. Attach each finding to its issue ID and a source the author can retrieve. Check the metric direction and units, comparator, data split, inference or training budget, tuning procedure and aggregation unit as relevant. For reported uncertainty, identify what varies across observations. For theory, check the assumptions that make the proof or counterexample apply.
2. Read paired conditions jointly. A complete-system comparison can support a system claim. An attribution claim needs a comparison that isolates the proposed change. Retain a mismatch that affects interpretation, including variations in labels, compute budget or the population evaluated.
3. Decide what to **retain, narrow, correct or leave open** for each claim. Report the condition where a gain appears and the condition where it disappears when both bear on the question. An inconclusive estimate may warrant a descriptive sentence. A failed run establishes its failure and leaves the scientific comparison open.
4. Preserve units, meaningful uncertainty and source ownership when incorporating collaborator results. If a table and its notes conflict, carry the uncontested findings forward and identify the exact conflicting cell or protocol for the author to resolve.

Return one row per claim with *issue ID, finding and provenance, supported wording, remaining condition or gap*. Give a response outline reflecting the actual findings. Keep pending work clearly marked. Reserve past-tense result language for completed work with a traceable source.
