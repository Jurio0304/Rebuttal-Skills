# 6. Follow up with reviewers and chairs

Start from the actual posted reply when available, the new comment, its audience and the remaining discussion window. A local draft is an acceptable working source when labeled as such. A simulated review can inform preparation. Use actual reviewer feedback when describing the discussion.

- Identify exactly what the reviewer accepted, what remains disputed and what is new. Answer the new question first. Cite or reproduce only the earlier evidence needed to understand this reply. If the reviewer acknowledges the answer and asks nothing further, closing the thread is appropriate.
- A substantive objection may require a correction, narrower claim, missing control or clarification of a prior result. When the evidence ends before the requested conclusion, state the remaining question and what the current evidence establishes.
- For a procedural issue, check the chair's role and the permitted channel. A short note can identify the dated events, their consequence and the specific clarification requested. Attribute a stated intention accurately. Treat silence as an absence of feedback. A reminder is appropriate only when the procedure, timing and outstanding need justify it.

Return the requested reply or chair note, with its audience identified outside the text. Preserve the distinction among the author's response, the reviewer's acknowledgement, a stated intention, and a change visible in the rating field. Record the source and date for any claimed change. A read receipt, a local draft and an assistant's simulation each have their own status. Update the thread record only as far as the supplied evidence supports.
